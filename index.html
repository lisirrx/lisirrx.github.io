      Research Experience

      Juicer: Proactive Ordering & Conflict Pacing

      Research Software EngineerSeptember 2024–present

      Planned submission: OSDI 2027

      This project investigates common causes of transaction aborts and how to address them across existing concurrency-control protocols, with an emphasis on easy adoption across different database systems.

      • Understanding aborts. We characterize contention-related aborts across locking, optimistic, and timestamp-based protocols, distinguishing cross-shard order inversions from conflicts involving requests, control messages, and transaction semantics. These conflicts can trigger aborts even when transactions arrive in a consistent order.
      • A horizontal design approach. We propose improving existing concurrency-control mechanisms through proactive message ordering and conflict pacing, rather than replacing them with a new protocol. This approach targets opportunities shared across different mechanisms while reducing the engineering effort required for adoption.
      • RPC-layer implementation. We implement the approach through gRPC interceptors that identify ordering hotspots, reorder transaction messages, and regulate their delivery according to message dependencies. The underlying concurrency-control mechanism remains responsible for correctness.

      Lakehouse Concurrency Control

      Research Software EngineerApril 2026–present

      Planned submission: OSDI 2027

      In lakehouse systems, a commit conflict can invalidate an entire write statement after expensive computation and object-store I/O have already completed. Retrying the statement repeats scans, computation, and S3 requests, even when only a small part of its work is affected.

      • Measuring wasted work. We build concurrent SQL workloads and statement-level instrumentation to attribute computation, object-store requests, and transferred bytes to aborted attempts, and investigate which conflicts can be resolved without full re-execution.
      • Ordering and incremental repair. We explore coordinating writers through early read/write intents, preserving unaffected outputs, and deferring work that depends on earlier conflicting writes. The goal is to make repair proportional to the affected data while retaining concurrency.
      • Spark/Iceberg prototype. We are implementing an advisory coordination service and connector changes for incremental repair at commit time, with validation against committed Iceberg snapshots. The current prototype focuses on a restricted class of MERGE statements; broader SQL coverage and end-to-end evaluation remain in progress.
